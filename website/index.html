Persistent Responsibilities for AI Agents

Give AI agents something to own beyond a single task — with persistent context, evidence, and decisions across runs.

Get started on GitHub See how it works →

01 · How responsibilities work

Responsibilities stay. Matters come and go.

Most agent work is a task: a prompt, a run, a result. Much of what matters isn’t. “Customers waiting on us get an answer within two working days” is never finished; it has to stay true. A responsibility says what should be true and who answers for it. An agent can take it on, run after run.

Cases come from facts. What the systems show, such as the support inbox or the payment system, is read against what is expected. Actions can change the situation; fresh evidence shows what changed.

Responsibility
What should stay true and who answers for it, written as expectations an agent can judge against. Versioned: each recorded decision names the version it was made under.
Judgment
A run judges new evidence against the expectation and what came before, and decides what follows. Its decisions are kept with the evidence behind them.
Case
One concrete situation, followed across runs until it is over: this customer, waiting for three days; this booking, still unpaid. A case can end without the expected outcome being achieved.
Attention
A situation that requires action. The agent can act on it itself, or pass it to another agent or a person. It can open early or late in a case, and close while the case goes on.

02 · Examples

Three responsibilities, three kinds of attention.

Each is something an agent can own: not a task to finish, but an expectation to keep true.

  1. Bookings are paid before the event.

    The invoicing automation sent the invoice and, by its own measure, succeeded. Ten days before the event the payment system still shows nothing received, so a case opens. A call clarifies it: the customer will pay on Friday. The matter is explained, but not over until the money arrives. If the customer cancels within the terms instead, it is over too, without a payment.

  2. Customers waiting on us get an answer within two working days.

    A request has waited three working days, so a case opens. The support system records a reply sent to the customer. That is evidence of an answer; a closed status alone is not. The wait is over, but the two-day target was missed.

  3. A significant revenue decline is understood and owned.

    One product’s monthly revenue has been falling for weeks, by an amount that matters. No one made a mistake and no task failed, but someone should understand it. It may be a price change the team chose, and they accept the lower level. It may be customers leaving, and they decide what to do. What ends this case, and what counts as a good outcome, depend on the expectations set beforehand.

03 · Delegation

Delegate the work. Keep the responsibility.

A responsibility is about the state, not about who works on it, so it holds when the work changes hands. Take the support responsibility above.

  1. You hand the routine replies to an agent. What you answer for stays the same.
  2. On Tuesday the agent runs out of tokens and stops. A customer who has just written in hears nothing.
  3. On Thursday the responsibility’s next run finds the request still waiting in the support system, whatever the agent reported or didn’t. A case comes to your attention.
  4. You reply and raise the agent’s budget. The support system records your reply, and the case closes.

You didn’t have to read every reply to catch this. That gives you a basis for handing over more, step by step, and improving the agent as you go.

04 · Architecture

Observe deterministically. Judge probabilistically. Remember persistently.

No fixed workflow, no dashboard to watch. In each run, the agent evaluates new evidence against the lasting expectation and everything known so far. Nothing has to run in between, and the next run needn’t be the same agent.

  1. Observe

    Signals run the same queries against the systems of record, every time. What they find is kept as evidence, and relevant new evidence can start the next run.

    Evidence

  2. Judge

    The agent evaluates new evidence against the expectation and its history, decides what matters, and determines what to do next. It can act through its host’s tools, involve another agent or a person, or decide that no action is needed.

    Decisions

  3. Remember

    The responsibility, its cases, the evidence they rest on and every decision carry over, so each judgment starts from what is already known.

    Context

05 · Built for developers

Four functions. One responsibility lifecycle.

Responsibilities Core is the open-source core of this concept. It is the decision step of each run, as four pure functions over plain JSON: deterministic TypeScript with no runtime dependencies. The same inputs give byte-identical outputs. It loads, stores, schedules, calls models and executes nothing, and has no clock: your code supplies the time. Your host handles scheduling, models, tools and persistence.

view
What is going on? Prepares what the agent sees: the observations, the cases, the expectations and the kinds of attention a case can need.
step
Does the proposal hold? Checks the agent’s proposals and returns the transitions, the new state and the Attentions to deliver.
command
Optional: a person decides. A human override that sets an existing case to open, resolved or dismissed.
assess
Was it a success? Checks a case against its success criteria: met, not met or unknown. Closed is not success.

See it run: in the simulation, Claude Code and Codex each judge one responsibility for six days — one fresh run a day, with no memory of their own. Each run starts from the responsibility’s state and the evidence so far, and that is enough. They open the case, hold back for three days, call in the support lead on day five when nothing has moved, and close that attention on day six once work resumes, leaving the case open. 06 · Accountability

Delegating the work does not automatically transfer accountability.

An agent can own a responsibility day to day; a person still answers for it. People define what should be true and what agents and automations may do on their own. Accountability can change hands through an explicit handover. Every case can be checked against the facts behind it.

A responsibility follows states in the world. Its view is only as current and complete as its observations. An agent acts only with the tools and permissions its host gives it, and steps that can’t be undone still need safeguards before they happen.
